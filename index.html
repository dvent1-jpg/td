Dennis Ventrello Tech Direct Business Review

Tech Direct Business Review · 2026

Turning 2025 insights
into 2026 profit

What's working, what's not, and what we can do
about it.

Dennis Ventrello External Business Advisor

2025 was profitable, but
there's a lot of opportunity
for growth

  • Product Sales Division was up $123K
  • Engineer Support Division was down $31K
  • Consultancy Support Division was down $529K
  • Revenue was down 2%, while costs rose 5%
  • Profit slipped from $1.49M to $1.05M, a 29% drop

Product Sales had a
good year, with help

  • Profit rose 34%, from $362K to $484K
  • Revenue rose 4% while costs held flat
  • Market share went from 17% to 18%, but grew less than others who were also able to take advantage of Home Secure's absence
  • Customers score it 9 out of 10 on speed and delivery, against an industry 7

Don't assume 2025 will repeat

Customers like buying from Sales, but they don't like the price. The market leader is back this year, and cheaper online sellers keep growing. Sales has to win on what competitors can't offer.

Consultancy Support
swung and missed

  • Profit fell 59%, from $903K to $375K, after rising 31% the year before
  • Revenue fell 16%, while costs rose 25%
  • Outside revenue fell 36%, while Consulting's marketing spend rose roughly 26%
  • Customers score it 5 out of 10 on understanding their needs, against an industry 8

Consulting is flashing red

Tech Direct built a cost base for a bigger Consulting business, and then outside customers stopped coming. Referrals from Sales and Engineering are hiding the decline.

Engineer Support is
slipping away

  • Profit fell 14%, from $224K to $193K. Now down 44% since 2023
  • Revenue rose 2% while costs rose 4%
  • Market share went from 17% to 16%, and repeat business is at just 45%, 21 to 28 points below national competitors
  • Annual turnover is 32%, against a 12% industry norm
  • Customers score engineers' product knowledge 4 out of 10, against an industry 8

It's a slow leak, not a collapse

Sales and Consulting keep sending Engineering customers, but Engineering has slipped a little every year on profit, on market share and on people. That makes it easy to miss.

Why it's happening

Positioned for convenience,
but providing complexity

  • A Sales customer who wants installation is “directed to speak with” Engineering
  • Consulting designs the system, then sends the customer back to Sales to buy it and on to Engineering to install it
  • Staff say they're “discouraged from contacting the other divisions”
  • Customers like the complete package, but call the coordination “confusing”

The customer is doing a lot of the work

Tech Direct has everything needed to take a customer from first call to working system. But the customer is still the one connecting the pieces.

Consulting's staffing model
is damaging two divisions

  • Tech Direct has “typically transferred Engineers” to fill Consulting vacancies
  • Consultants train 18 days a year, double the norm, but employees say the emphasis is technical rather than customer-facing
  • Customers are noticing. “I was expecting a Consultant to discuss my business needs not an Engineer”
  • Turnover is 30% in Consulting and 32% in Engineering, against a 12% norm

Consulting vacancies are paid for twice

When a consultant leaves and an engineer fills the gap, Tech Direct is effectively turning one problem into two separate ones, and additional training has been unable to fix this problem.

Measuring the parts, but
not considering the whole

  • Targets are set by division, seemingly without any shared customer level measurements
  • Customers are surveyed three times, by division, and nobody asks about the whole job
  • Yet 68% of Consulting's revenue and 78% of Engineering's comes from inside Tech Direct
  • There's no clear view of what one customer is worth across the entire funnel

Customer value has no obvious owner

Tech Direct knows what each division earns, but not how customers and staff move between them. Staff are discouraged from crossing those lines, but most of the revenue depends on it anyway.

Where Tech Direct goes from here

Why Tech Direct is uniquely
positioned for growth

  • Tech Direct sells, designs and installs, while its competitors compete as specialists in individual markets
  • Customers want the “complete package” when making a security purchase
  • The divisions already feed each other, generating meaningful demand from internal referrals
  • Engineers are nationwide, on call, and will service equipment bought from anyone, so a competitor's customer can still become Tech Direct's
  • Sales consistently scores high on customer interactions, and Tech Direct trains above the norm in every division

Convenience is the differentiator

Tech Direct isn't the cheapest, and it doesn't need to be, because it can be the easiest. The pieces are already in place, they just need to work together.

Why restraint matters, and
what not to do yet

  • Merging the three divisions. Fixing the handoffs comes first, and a reorganization is expensive, disruptive and not yet justified
  • Cutting prices to fight online sellers. Price pressure is real, but there's no evidence the added volume would cover the lost margin
  • Spending more on Consulting marketing. Marketing already went up while outside revenue fell
  • Copying competitors' Engineering models. Tech Direct already trains above the norm and offers longer contracts, so the real question is why customers and staff don't stay
  • Shifting toward commercial customers. They're more profitable, but the gain is modest next to the bigger opportunities already visible

Cheap, reversible, short-term
opportunities

  • Any new Consultant should be selected for product knowledge and customer skills. Sourcing from Engineering by default ends
  • Consider new Consultants and Engineers starting with structured time alongside Sales in St. Louis
  • Rebalance Consulting training toward client discovery, business needs, and customer conversations
  • Book the next step on the same call. An installation customer leaves a Sales call with a date
  • Before leaving the customer, the Consultant places the order and books the install through the contact center
  • Drop the norm that discourages divisions from contacting each other, and start counting leads passed

Structural, but contained
mid-term opportunities

  • Create clearer progression paths where turnover is highest, and use senior roles to strengthen mentoring for new hires
  • Pilot Sales as an additional source of Consulting talent, especially for employees interested in field based roles
  • Turn the manual handoff into a connected workflow, giving Consultants live access to products, pricing and Engineering availability
  • Begin tracking shared metrics, including repeat business, end of job satisfaction, revenue by customer and cross division conversion
  • Test the model in one region for six months, with the rest of the business as the comparison

Long-term opportunities, and
the retention growth engine

  • Use the new customer level metrics to decide where to invest across Sales, Consulting and Engineering
  • Create a single customer entry point. One number, and one team that routes and books whichever service the customer needs
  • Extend digital beyond product sales. One online path to book a consultation, an installation, a repair or a maintenance visit
  • Test a way to win customers who buy their equipment elsewhere. Installation gets Tech Direct in the door, and maintenance builds the relationship
  • Improve contract take rate by reworking Engineering service contracts around what keeps customers coming back

Appendix

Open questions

  • How many customers use more than one division, and what is one customer worth across all three?
  • Where do customers drop out between divisions?
  • What drives turnover in Consulting and Engineering, and where do those people go?
  • Why is Engineering's repeat business only 45%, and what separates the customers who stay?
  • What do Engineering's longer contracts include today, and why do only 52% of clients take one?
  • What did Consulting's $116K technology spend buy, and can it support connected handoffs?
  • How does pay in Consulting and Engineering compare with competitors?
